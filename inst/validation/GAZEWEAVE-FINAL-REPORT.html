GazeWeave · final exploratory report

What recognition gaze contains—and what it does not

A response-blind decomposition of participant-specific study gaze, other-participant image templates, new-item controls, saliency, density, item effects, and 1–4 newness ratings in the Wynn probe-delay dataset.

Analysis date: 21 August 2026 · 4 crossed outer folds · 2,000 participant/item bootstrap draws · exact visibility masks deferred

The detectable signal is image identity, not participant-specific reinstatement. Transport ranks the correct study item above nonmatches. But the same signal appears when the participant’s own study gaze is replaced by other people’s gaze, and it remains on newtest trials where the participant has no study memory for the item. The own-template advantage is uncertain and neither component predicts graded recognition confidence out of sample.

1. The decomposition that resolves the puzzle

The canonical method is now called Transport; “v3” appears only in historical checkpoint names and denotes the edge-normalized, four-episode development estimand. Five candidate items were scored on every trial. Each candidate used the participant’s four study presentations in the own condition, or the same four presentation numbers drawn deterministically from other participants who saw the exact same item version in the group condition.

Their difference is −0.69 points (CI −6.08 to 3.95). This is the same story as the margin: item identity is weakly recoverable, but personal encoding history adds no reliable ranking benefit.

2. Does Transport beat “dumb” density?

Not reliably. Transport has the larger point estimates, but the paired uncertainty intervals do not establish superiority over the simple σ=80 density comparator.

Why the new margin result does not contradict the frozen court. Candidate margin and top-one rank ask whether the true item tends to outrank four nonmatches. Proper information also penalizes weak or miscalibrated probabilities. Transport has a small ranking signal, but it is not strong enough to yield a secure proper-score gain—or a secure advantage over density.

3. The bare saliency diagnostic

There is no stable saliency effect in this dataset under the current crossed inference. Across the 20–100 saliency manipulation, adjusted Transport item information changes by 0.0240 bits (95% CI −0.0534 to 0.0963). For old probes alone the estimate is 0.0373 bits (−0.0746 to 0.1404); for lures it is 0.0120 bits (−0.0952 to 0.1192). Density σ=80 is likewise null overall and by probe type.

This report concerns the Wynn probe-delay study. The previously discussed significant old-probe density/saliency slope belonged to the separate PCMRI catalog reconstruction and is not evidence for this dataset.

5. Item effects

Some images do generate more consistent gaze organization than others, especially across repeated study viewings. That regularity does not transfer reliably to recognition.

The positive study item effect is therefore best described as image-driven viewing structure during repeated exposure, not as a stable item propensity for recognition reinstatement. None of the item propensities improved held-out oldness prediction.

6. What we were missing

  • The original proper-score endpoint was too coarse to reveal the weak ranking signal. Candidate margin and top-one inference show that the correct item is identifiable above chance.
  • Identity is not the same as reinstatement. Other observers identify the item nearly as well, and newtest trials retain the signal. The likely source is shared image structure, object layout, or generic viewing behavior.
  • The behavior endpoint correctly stays null. Once common image evidence is modeled, participant-specific Transport evidence contributes essentially no held-out confidence information.
  • Broad saliency percentage is not the same as the exact information available on a trial. Exact masks could separate visible-region attraction from hidden-region reinstatement, but that is a new measurement project and was intentionally excluded here.

7. Scope, safeguards, and claims

Response-blind measurement

Donor selection, candidate scoring, and the sealed measurement summary were completed before opening the 1–4 responses.

Donor integrity

Zero self-donor episodes; five candidates and four presentation episodes throughout. Sparse item/version cells used 2–4 distinct donors per candidate.

Private-data boundary

Trial-linked predictions and raw Wynn data remain local and Git-ignored. The report contains aggregates only.

Permitted Transport detects a small item-identification signal in recognition gaze; repeated viewing contains image-specific structure; the canonical method remains scientifically interpretable and computationally usable.

Not permitted Participant-specific recognition reinstatement, superiority over density, a saliency-dependent rescue, confidence prediction, or a stable recognition item effect.
